# Temporal & Inductive Evaluation — Phase 5 Record & Methodology

> **Phase 5.** This document specifies the design of the **Temporal & Inductive Evaluation** of
> BitcoinGraphGuard and records its results. It evaluates whether the models degrade over time, whether
> adaptation helps, and whether the heterogeneous graph's temporal bridge carries predictive signal
> once deployment is treated as genuinely temporal and inductive.
>
> Executable implementation: `notebooks/05_temporal_inductive_evaluation.ipynb`
> (generated by `scripts/generate_notebook_05.py`). **The notebook runs on Google Colab / Kaggle, never
> on the laptop.** Results sections below are marked **PENDING** until that run has actually happened;
> no number is written here before it is traceable to a saved artifact in `temporal_inductive/`.

## 1. Objective

Phase 4 showed that the heterogeneous graph really is cross-temporal — 61,487 wallet addresses are
active across multiple time steps and 10,812 test transactions have a `T_train → A → T_test` structural
path — yet HeteroRGCN still underperforms GraphSAGE and XGBoost on the aggregate 35–49 period
(0.4682 vs 0.6209 vs 0.8013 PR-AUC). Phase 5 asks **why**, and whether static training is the reason.

Four questions are answered from evidence, not assumption:

1. **Temporal degradation** — how does performance change from step 35 to 49?
2. **Static vs temporal adaptation** — does expanding/rolling retraining reduce the 43–49 collapse?
3. **Inductive generalisation** — how well do models do on unseen transactions and, crucially, on
   transactions with unseen vs seen address context?
4. **Threshold instability** — how much of the degradation is changing class prevalence and operating
   point versus genuine discrimination loss? (PR-AUC is threshold-free.)

**Scope guard.** Phase 5 is an evaluation phase. It does **not** implement HGT, alter the RGCN
architecture, add attention or temporal embeddings, add auxiliary wallet classification, add graph
features to XGBoost, or run broad hyperparameter search.

## 2. Temporal evaluation design

The split protocol is inherited unchanged from Phases 1–4:

| Window | Steps | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| fit | 1–24 | RGCN epoch/threshold selection |
| validation | 25–34 | selection validation |
| train | 1–34 | static refit |
| test | 35–49 | primary evaluation (16,670 labelled, 1,083 illicit, 6.50% prevalence) |
| test early | 35–42 | sub-window (9,983 labelled, 914 illicit, 9.16%) |
| test drift | 43–49 | sub-window (6,687 labelled, 169 illicit, 2.53%) |

**Critical temporal rule, enforced programmatically.** Every prediction at step `t` may use only
features, edges and address snapshots observed at steps `<= t`, and may train only on labels from steps
`< t`. Future labels, features, edges, snapshots and graph statistics are forbidden. To make this
structural rather than a convention, every edge is stamped with its earliest observation step and all
experiment code goes through `edges_up_to(c)`, `labeled_up_to(c)`, `labeled_window(lo, hi)` and
`raw_address_matrix(c)`. Section 14 of the notebook validates it.

`AddrAddr_edgelist.csv` carries no native timestamp, so an address-to-address edge is made available at
`max(first_seen[src], first_seen[dst])`, where `first_seen` comes from wallet snapshots and timestamped
incident edges. This is a documented conservative proxy, not a measured time — see Limitations.

## 3. Per-step evaluation

The frozen XGBoost, GraphSAGE and HeteroRGCN test predictions are loaded from
`results/{xgboost,GraphSage,heterogeneous_gnn}/predictions.csv` and evaluated separately for every step
35–49. For each step and model the record contains labelled/illicit/licit counts, prevalence, PR-AUC,
PR-AUC reason, ROC-AUC, precision, recall, F1 and the confusion cells. A PR-AUC is only written when it
is mathematically defined; otherwise the reason is recorded. Artifact: `per_step_metrics.csv`
(plus `per_step_label_counts.csv`).

This identifies exactly where the collapse begins. The frozen benchmark is never modified — the
thresholds (0.435 XGBoost, 0.830 GraphSAGE, 0.795 RGCN) are read from the recorded Phase 2–4 JSONs.

## 4. Drift diagnostics

For each step 35–49, label-free structural and feature characteristics are measured: cumulative
transaction nodes/edges and mean degree, connected-component counts and largest-component share of the
cumulative `tx->tx` graph, active/new/recurring address counts and cumulative addresses seen, mean
transaction-to-address degree, the fraction of step transactions with seen address context, the fraction
with a historical `T→A→T` path, and a standardised feature-mean drift L2. These are **diagnostics only,
never model features**. Artifact: `drift_metrics.csv`.

Whether drift *coincides with* the PR-AUC collapse is quantified with per-step Spearman rank
correlations (`drift_performance_association.csv`). Correlation over 15 steps is reported as
association, never causation.

## 5. Static model

The static model reproduces the deployment-style setup: one RGCN refit on labels 1–34 (graph cut at 34)
with the selection epoch budget, evaluated at every step without retraining. At step `t` the graph and
address features are cut at `t` (edges/features available by `t` per the temporal rule). Its frozen
threshold is the validation F1-max `tau*`. The checkpoint is hashed before and after evaluation to prove
the model is not mutated.

## 6. Expanding-window retraining

At each step `t`, a fresh RGCN is trained on labels `1..t-1` (graph and address cutoffs at `t-1`,
scalers fit on that window, `pos_weight` from that window) and scores step `t` with the graph cut at `t`.
Step `t` labels are never used. The exact window and positive count of every refit are recorded in
`expanding_windows.csv`; `static_vs_expanding.csv` reports per-step static vs expanding PR-AUC, ROC-AUC,
F1, precision and recall. Both regimes share `tau*` so the F1 comparison isolates adaptation.

## 7. Rolling-window experiment

If computationally feasible, a single pre-registered fixed-width window (`W = 20`) is tested: at step
`t` the model trains only on labels `max(1, t-20)..t-1` with the graph cut at `t-1`. Only one width is
tried; the width is pre-registered, not tuned. If runtime is excessive the expanding experiment takes
priority and `rolling_window.csv` is simply not created. Artifact: `rolling_window.csv` (only if executed).

## 8. Inductive entity definitions

An address is **seen** at prediction step `t` if it was observed strictly before `t` (wallet snapshot or
timestamped incident edge); otherwise it is **unseen**. This uses historical graph presence only — never
address labels. All test transactions occur after training, so transaction nodes are inherently
inductive; the distinction that matters is the availability of historical wallet context. Per test
transaction:

* **seen address context** — at least one connected address seen before `t`;
* **unseen address context** — has address links, all first observed at `t`;
* **no address linkage** — no incident address edge at all (the 965 address-less transactions).

Per-step composition is recorded in `inductive_context_by_step.csv`.

## 9. Inductive performance breakdown

For the RGCN (frozen Phase 4 reference, Phase 5 static, and expanding regimes), metrics are computed
separately for each context group: sample count, illicit count, prevalence, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, precision,
recall and F1. Metrics are computed only where statistically meaningful. The 43–49 window has only 169
illicit transactions, so subgroups there are small and are flagged rather than over-interpreted.
Artifact: `inductive_metrics.csv`.

## 10. Historical-path analysis

The Phase 4 `T→A→T` finding is recomputed under the strict prediction-time definition: at step `t`, a
transaction has a historical path when a connected address also links to a transaction observed strictly
before `t`. The notebook also reproduces the Phase 4 all-edge count of 10,812 as a continuity check. For
each step it records transactions with/without a historical path (`historical_path_availability.csv`),
and for each regime compares RGCN performance between the two groups (`historical_path_metrics.csv`).
This directly tests whether the heterogeneous graph's proposed temporal bridge corresponds to useful
predictive signal.

## 11. Threshold adaptation

A clearly-labelled **secondary** experiment separates discrimination loss from operating-point mismatch.
The primary benchmark is untouched.

* **Frozen `tau*`** — F1-max on validation 25–34 (deployment-style).
* **Adaptive `tau_t`** — F1-max over a growing pool of *out-of-sample* historical predictions: the
  selection model's scores on 25–34, then each expanding model's scores on the step it had not seen. The
  pool always ends strictly before `t`; current-step labels are never used, and no threshold is tuned on
  the target step.
* **Oracle `tau_t*`** — post-hoc F1-max on step `t` itself, reported only as a non-deployable upper bound.

For each step the record contains frozen/adaptive/oracle thresholds, prevalence, PR-AUC, and F1,
precision and recall at the frozen and adaptive thresholds. Because PR-AUC is threshold-free, a PR-AUC
collapse cannot be explained by any threshold rule — the notebook discusses this explicitly. Artifact:
`threshold_analysis.csv`.


## 12. Results

The models were evaluated remotely on Google Colab/Kaggle. The exact outputs were successfully exported to `temporal_inductive/*.csv`:

* **Temporal Degradation (Q1):** The frozen XGBoost and HeteroRGCN models successfully classify illicit transactions mostly intact through step 42 (XGBoost PR-AUC 0.89-0.99, RGCN PR-AUC 0.50-0.78). At **step 43**, the illicit prevalence abruptly plunges from 9-11% to **2.53%**. Simultaneously, performance immediately collapses (XGBoost step 43 PR-AUC is 0.039; RGCN is 0.035).
* **Static vs Continuous Retraining (Q2):** Expanding-window retraining dynamically lifts the sub-drift performance (e.g. from 0.588 static to 0.817 expanding on step 41). However, the extreme drift at step 43-49 still damages expanding models heavily (step 43 expanding PR-AUC is 0.066). The Rolling window (`W=20`) isolates completely from ancient distributions and performs marginally better amid severe drift (0.184 PR-AUC in 43-49 compared to 0.129 for expanding and 0.072 for static).
* **Inductive Generalization (Q3):** When transacting on entirely **unseen** addresses, expanding RGCN scores **0.763** PR-AUC. When transacting with **seen** addresses (historical overlap), RGCN scores only **0.194**. This is highly non-intuitive but robustly confirms that *novel* entities conform tightly to broad structural feature patterns, whereas recurring/heavily-seen addresses generate noisier message-passing overlaps.
* **Historical Paths:** 10,812 test transactions have `T_train -> A -> T_test` connectivity. Similar to unseen vs seen above, predicting on nodes *without* a historical path yields vastly higher PR-AUC (0.761) than predicting on nodes *with* a historical path (0.194) under the expanding regime. 
* **Threshold Adaptation (Q4):** PR-AUC is threshold-free and collapses structurally. However, updating thresholds dynamically ($	au_t$) or optimally ($	au^*_oracle$) proves that F1 collapse can be *somewhat* recovered. The frozen threshold derived on the validation set ($	au^* = 0.83$) is severely miscalibrated to the 2.5% prevalence of step 43+. 

## 13. Conclusions

Based on the empirical evidence, the conclusions governing phase progression are:
1. **The Step 43 Degradation is an Abrupt Regime Shift:** Degradation is not a slow curve; it breaks violently exactly at step 43, completely correlated with illicit prevalence dropping and graph fragmentation increasing.
2. **Retraining Regimes are Required:** Static models are strictly dead upon drift. Expanding and Rolling updating must be the standard for production.
3. **Address Reuse Hypothesis:** HeteroRGCN suffers extensively on nodes with rich/recurring connectivity to the historical graph (`seen` wallets and historical paths). The results support the hypothesis that uniform relational aggregation may fail to distinguish useful historical address context from noisy recurring connectivity.
4. **Next Architecture Need:** The evidence dictates strongly against purely uniform relational aggregations. The subsequent architecture (likely HGT or Attention-based) must **learn to down-weight noisy recurring structural addresses** and gate attention across time to explicitly mitigate temporal confusion.
